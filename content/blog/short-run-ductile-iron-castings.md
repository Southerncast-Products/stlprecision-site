A short run of ductile iron castings, anywhere from a single piece to a few hundred, is ordinary work for a sand casting foundry built for it. The questions that decide price and success are which ASTM A536 grade the part needs, how the pattern tooling is handled across a small quantity, and whether the drawing package gives the foundry enough to quote it right the first time. St. Louis Precision Cast Products pours MEEHANITE® ductile iron in runs of 1 to 500 pieces from our foundry in St. Louis, Missouri, and this is what to expect.

## Who buys ductile iron in short runs

Most short-run ductile work comes from three places:

- **Replacement parts.** A housing, bracket, hub or lever on older equipment has cracked or worn out, and the original supplier no longer makes it.
- **Specialized equipment.** Machine builders whose annual volume for a given part is in the tens or low hundreds, not the thousands a high-volume line wants.
- **Prototypes before production.** An engineering team that needs real castings in the real material to test before committing to production tooling.

In all three cases, the buyer needs ductile iron's strength and toughness, and the quantity is too small to interest a foundry set up for long production runs.

## Choosing the ASTM A536 grade

Ductile iron is specified under ASTM A536. Each grade name gives three minimums: tensile strength in ksi, yield strength in ksi, and elongation in percent. So 65-45-12 means at least 65,000 psi tensile, 45,000 psi yield and 12% elongation.

| ASTM A536 grade | Tensile (ksi, min) | Yield (ksi, min) | Elongation (%, min) | Where it tends to be used |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 60-40-18 | 60 | 40 | 18 | Parts that must tolerate shock and distortion: brackets, housings, fittings |
| 65-45-12 | 65 | 45 | 12 | The general-purpose grade: machinery frames, gear housings, equipment brackets |
| 80-55-06 | 80 | 55 | 6 | Higher loads where less elongation is acceptable: hubs, levers, gears |
| 100-70-03 | 100 | 70 | 3 | Heavily loaded parts where strength governs |
| 120-90-02 | 120 | 90 | 2 | Very highly stressed parts with little impact demand |

The trade-off runs in one direction: as strength goes up, elongation comes down. A higher grade is not automatically a better part. If the casting takes impact or could be overloaded in service, the extra elongation of 60-40-18 or 65-45-12 is often worth more than extra tensile strength.

If you are replacing an existing part and do not know the grade, say so. A sample, the service conditions and any failure history are enough to start the conversation. If you are still deciding between ductile and gray iron, our guide on [ductile iron vs gray iron](/blog/ductile-vs-gray-iron) walks through that choice.

As a MEEHANITE® licensed foundry, we pour controlled ductile irons with a specified graphite structure. That matters most when the properties need to be consistent from one casting to the next, which is exactly the situation in a repeat order for a part that has to fit and perform like the last batch.

## What pattern tooling does to a short run

Every sand casting starts with a pattern, and often core boxes for internal passages. On a long production run, that tooling cost disappears into the piece price. On a short run, it is usually the single biggest line on the first quote.

The arithmetic is simple. Tooling is a one-time charge spread over the quantity you buy. Whatever the pattern costs, it is the whole cost at one piece, a tenth of it per part at ten pieces, and a small fraction at a few hundred. That is why the same casting can look expensive as a single piece and very reasonable as a run of 100.

A few things keep short-run tooling sensible:

- **Plan for the repeat order.** If you expect to buy the part again, a pattern that is stored and maintained makes every later order cheaper. We offer pattern storage and maintenance for repeat orders.
- **Ask for tooling as its own line.** It makes the quote easier to compare, and it makes clear who owns the pattern.
- **Mention existing tooling.** If a pattern already exists from a previous supplier, photos and its condition can change the quote.
- **Separate the prototype from production.** Tell the foundry how many pieces you need now and how many you expect later. The right tooling for 5 prototypes and the right tooling for 300 a year are not always the same.

## How a short run is made here

Our molding is no-bake, air-set sand. Chemically bonded sand molds hold their shape well, which gives good surface finish and dimensional accuracy without the dedicated equipment a high-volume molding line needs. That is a large part of why short runs are practical.

Before the first pour, we run MAGMA solidification simulation to check how the casting will fill and freeze. On a short run, there is no long production ramp to tune the process over, so catching a shrinkage or feeding problem in software before the first casting is worth more than it would be on a long run.

We pour castings up to 2,000 lbs, do stress relieving and annealing in house, and supply castings ready for machining. Our [quality process](/quality) covers dimensional inspection, chemical analysis, mechanical property verification and full traceability and certification.

## What to send for a quote

The fastest quotes come from complete packages. Send what you have of the following:

1. **A drawing or 3D model.** STEP files are ideal. A 2D drawing with tolerances, datums and machined surfaces marked is just as important.
2. **The material callout,** for example "ASTM A536 65-45-12", and any heat treatment.
3. **Quantity now and quantity later.** "10 now, roughly 100 a year after that" quotes very differently from "10 total".
4. **Which surfaces are machined,** so the casting can be made with the right stock on them.
5. **Testing and certification you need,** such as hardness, tensile test or a material certification per heat.
6. **Service conditions,** like load, temperature, impact or wear, especially if you are not sure of the grade.
7. **For a replacement part with no drawing:** photos, overall dimensions, weight, and whether a physical sample can be sent.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is there a minimum order for ductile iron castings?**
No. We run short production from a single piece up to 500 pieces per run. The quote for one piece will carry most of the tooling cost, so it helps to tell us if more will follow.

**Can you match the grade of a part I am replacing?**
Usually, yes, if you know the original specification. If you do not, the service conditions and the part itself are the starting point for recommending an ASTM A536 grade.

**Do you pour other irons besides ductile?**
Yes. We also pour MEEHANITE® gray iron, abrasion resistant white irons (NiHard, ASTM A532), Ni-Resist irons (ASTM A439 D-2 and D-5), carbon and low alloy steels, tool steels, stainless steel and bronze. The full list is on [the metals we pour](/materials).

**Can I order prototypes first and production later?**
Yes. Rapid prototype castings in the production material are one of our [core services](/services), and pattern tooling can be stored for the production order.
